# "Slower Than Molasses in January" — original script v1 (2026-11-17)
Format: cartoon 3D story short, ~68 s at ~3.7 words/s, one steady narrator, no captions, English.

## Facts (with sources)
- 15 Jan 1919, about 12:30 p.m., Purity Distilling Co. tank (owned by US Industrial Alcohol), 529 Commercial St, Boston North End, by the harbour. [Wikipedia: Great Molasses Flood; Live Science 2016]
- Tank 50 ft (15 m) tall (~5 storeys), 90 ft wide, 2.3 million US gallons (~12,000 t). [Wikipedia]
- Leaked from the day it was first filled (1915); painted brown to hide the leaks. [Wikipedia; Live Science]
- Neighbourhood children brought pails and filled them with leaking molasses. [New England Historical Society]
- Built under treasurer Arthur Jell, no engineering background; skipped tests; tank "shuddered and groaned" when filled. [Wikipedia; NEHS]
- Days before: a ship delivered a fresh, warmed load; air had risen from 2 °F to 41 °F. [Wikipedia; Live Science]
- Witnesses: rivets popping out "sounded like machine gun fire". [NEHS]
- Wave up to ~25 ft, ~35 mph; steel panels hit the Atlantic Avenue elevated railway and tipped a car off its track. [Wikipedia]
- 21 dead, ~150 injured. [Wikipedia; Live Science]
- That night the temperature fell below freezing; Harvard fluid-dynamics study (Sharp, Kennedy, Rubinstein, APS DFD 2016): viscosity rose 4x or more, trapping victims and slowing rescuers. [Live Science]
- 2014 analysis: steel half as thick as it should have been. [Wikipedia]
- USIA blamed anarchists; court-appointed auditor found USIA responsible, $628,000. Laws then required a licensed architect and engineer to oversee such construction. [Wikipedia]
- Harbour stayed brown until summer. [Wikipedia]
- "Slower than molasses in January" is a long-standing American idiom.

## Script (253 words)
In 1919, a wave of molasses chased people down a street in Boston, faster than they could run.
It's January, and by the harbor stands a tank five stories tall, holding over two million gallons of syrup.
The tank leaks from day one, so the company fixes it the only way they know. They paint it brown.
Neighborhood kids bring pails and collect the drips for free.
Then a ship tops it up with a fresh load of warm molasses, and the steel starts to groan.
The man in charge isn't an engineer, so groaning sounds fine to him.
Just after lunch, the rivets shoot out like machine gun fire, and the whole tank bursts open.
A brown wave twenty-five feet high tears down the street at thirty-five miles an hour, and slams into the elevated railway.
There's an old saying: slower than molasses in January.
Luckily, it was January.
Unluckily, nobody told the molasses.
You see, molasses only crawls when it's cold.
Ever poured honey straight from the fridge? Warm it up, and it runs like juice.
That warm load made it runny and heavy, and the steel was half as thick as it should have been.
Then that night it froze, and the syrup set like glue around the rescuers.
Twenty-one people never made it out, and the company blamed anarchists.
The court blamed the company, and Massachusetts ruled that big tanks need a real engineer's signature.
And the harbor stayed brown until summer, which, to be fair, finally matched the tank.
